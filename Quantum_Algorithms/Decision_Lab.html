01 / The motivation

Why are we developing this software?

Because simply running an algorithm does not explain when it works, why it fails, or whether it offers a meaningful trade-off against classical approaches.

The problem

Optimization is difficult

Weighted Max-Cut asks us to split graph vertices into two groups, maximizing the sum of weights on edges crossing the partition. There are exponentially many assignments.

The research need

Algorithms require evidence

QAOA's outcomes depend on circuit depth, angles, graph structure, and optimizer behavior. We need controlled comparisons, not isolated screenshots.

Our response

Build an explainable testbed

Implement, independently validate, visualize, and benchmark QAOA and classical methods under documented experimental budgets.

What the software will produce

INPUT

Graph topology, edge weights, depth, parameters, random seeds

COMPUTE

Exact classical cut scores, quantum state evolution, optimization

OUTPUT

Expected cut, approximation ratio, resource counts, uncertainty, failures

03 / Existing work

If QAOA already exists, what is the point?

QAOA was introduced by Farhi, Goldstone, and Gutmann in 2014. Qiskit and OpenQAOA already support QAOA workflows. Parameter transfer and graph-aware strategies have also been studied. Our contribution cannot be “we implemented QAOA for the first time.”

06 / How we would evaluate the software

From visual demonstration to scientific evidence

Prove the mathematics

Derive HC = ½ Σ wij(I − ZiZj) and verify that basis-state eigenvalues equal classical cut values.

Verify the implementation

Check normalization, Hermiticity, single-edge analytical expectations, and agreement between an independent NumPy implementation and a quantum SDK.

Define fair comparisons

Use exact enumeration on small instances, random-cut expectation, and competent classical heuristics. Keep optimizer evaluation budgets equal where appropriate.

Measure outcomes

Expected approximation ratio, best sampled cut (separately), objective evaluations, wall-clock time, gate counts, simulation memory, and variance across seeds.

Audit prior work and interpret limits

Investigate whether graph-informed parameter initialization adds anything beyond established transfer methods; publish negative findings when supported.

09 / Questions the guide may ask

Defensible answers without exaggeration

“Why not just use Qiskit?”

We may use Qiskit, but our deliverable is a verified comparative study. An independent reference implementation and controlled experiments make the scientific argument auditable.

“Is this new research?”

The basic algorithm is not new. We propose a rigorous course-project evaluation and will only claim a novel result after reviewing relevant prior work and obtaining supporting evidence.

“Why no quantum hardware?”

QT789_D is a Quantum Algorithms course project. Software simulation permits exact small-instance verification and controlled experiments within four months.

“Will it beat classical algorithms?”

Unknown. We will compare fairly and report negative results. Simulated performance does not establish practical quantum speedup.
