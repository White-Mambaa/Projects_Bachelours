01 / Why this project

What problem are we trying to solve?

Suppose a network must be divided into two groups. Every connection crossing the boundary contributes value. Max-Cut asks for the division with the largest total value of crossing connections. The number of candidate assignments doubles with each added vertex, so exact brute-force enumeration quickly becomes costly.

Classical perspective

Enumerate partitions on small graphs to obtain a provably optimal cut. Compare larger problems using classical heuristics. These approaches provide a necessary reference point.

Quantum perspective

Prepare a parameterized quantum state whose measurement distribution favors certain partitions. A classical optimizer adjusts the circuit angles to improve the expected cut value.

Stakeholder interpretation: This project investigates a quantum-inspired computational methodology using a classical simulator. It does not assume or promise a quantum speedup.

04 / Mathematical foundation

From Max-Cut to a quantum circuit

For an undirected weighted graph G = (V, E, w), a binary assignment z labels the two sides of the partition. The Pauli-Z operator makes the classical cut value an eigenvalue of a diagonal cost Hamiltonian.

Classical objective

C(z) = Σ(i,j)∈E wij(zi ⊕ zj)

Each crossing edge contributes its weight; each non-crossing edge contributes zero.

Cost Hamiltonian

HC = ½ Σ(i,j)∈E wij(I − ZiZj)

HC|z⟩ = C(z)|z⟩ for every computational basis state.

Mixing Hamiltonian

HB = Σj=1n Xj

The mixer rotates amplitudes among candidate assignments.

QAOA objective

Fp(γ,β) = ⟨ψp|HC|ψp⟩

Maximize the expected cut, then examine sampled candidate solutions.

Sign convention: The cost unitary is exp(−iγHC) and the mixer is exp(−iβHB). For one unit-weight edge at p = 1, F = ½[1 + sin(4β)sin(γ)]. This identity is a verification target for the live simulator.

PROJECT PROPOSAL

Design, Mathematical Analysis, and Software-Based Evaluation of the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm for Combinatorial Optimization

Course: Quantum Algorithms (QT789_D)
Course Level: 7
Project Duration: Four Months
Project Domain: Quantum Algorithms, Computer Science, and Computational Optimization
Implementation Mode: Software-Based Quantum Simulation
Project Status: Proposed — Subject to Faculty Guide Approval

1. Abstract

Quantum algorithms provide alternative computational approaches to problems involving search, optimization, and mathematical computation. Among these, the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) is a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm designed to obtain approximate solutions to combinatorial optimization problems.

This project proposes a rigorous mathematical and software-based investigation of QAOA, using the Max-Cut problem as the primary optimization benchmark. The study will formulate the optimization problem using quantum Hamiltonians, construct parameterized quantum circuits, implement classical simulation of quantum state evolution, and evaluate the algorithm under different problem instances, circuit depths, and optimization strategies.

The research will also compare QAOA against appropriate classical optimization baselines, examining approximation quality, computational resource requirements, parameter sensitivity, and algorithmic limitations.

The project will be implemented entirely through software-based simulation without requiring physical quantum computing hardware. The theoretical framework will be grounded in eight prescribed quantum computation and quantum algorithms textbooks.

The expected outcome is a reproducible computational research framework, a mathematically supported analysis of QAOA, and a systematic evaluation of its strengths and limitations for selected combinatorial optimization problems.

2. Introduction and Motivation

Combinatorial optimization problems arise across computer science, artificial intelligence, machine learning, scheduling, network analysis, and resource allocation. Many such problems are computationally challenging, particularly as the number of decision variables increases.

Quantum computing introduces computational models based on superposition, unitary transformations, interference, and quantum measurement. Quantum optimization algorithms attempt to exploit these principles to explore structured optimization problems.

QAOA provides a suitable setting for investigating the relationship between quantum algorithm design and classical optimization. Its performance depends on the problem Hamiltonian, parameterized quantum circuit, circuit depth, and classical parameter optimization procedure.

A systematic study of these components is valuable for understanding both the theoretical capabilities and practical computational limitations of variational quantum algorithms.

3. Problem Statement

Although QAOA provides a general framework for approximate combinatorial optimization, its performance is sensitive to circuit depth, parameter selection, problem structure, and computational resource constraints.

The central problem addressed in this project is:

How do quantum circuit depth, optimization parameters, and graph structure influence the solution quality and computational efficiency of QAOA for combinatorial optimization problems?

The study will use weighted and unweighted Max-Cut instances to investigate this question through mathematical analysis and reproducible simulation.

4. Project Objectives

  1. Establish the mathematical foundations of QAOA using linear algebra, quantum operators, unitary transformations, and measurement theory.
  2. Formulate Max-Cut as a quantum optimization problem using an appropriate cost Hamiltonian.
  3. Design and implement parameterized QAOA circuits through software-based quantum simulation.
  4. Investigate the effect of circuit depth, variational parameters, and graph structure on optimization performance.
  5. Evaluate alternative classical parameter optimization and initialization strategies.
  6. Compare QAOA against exact solutions and appropriate classical optimization methods.
  7. Analyze algorithmic complexity, circuit resources, computational scalability, and practical limitations.
  8. Produce a reproducible experimental framework and comprehensive technical report.

5. Research Questions

RQ1: How can weighted Max-Cut be formulated and analyzed through the Hamiltonian representation used by QAOA?

RQ2: How does increasing QAOA circuit depth affect approximation quality and computational cost for selected graph families?

RQ3: How sensitive is QAOA performance to the choice of initial parameters and classical optimization method?

RQ4: Can graph structural information assist parameter initialization or transfer between related optimization instances?

RQ5: Under controlled computational budgets, how does simulated QAOA compare with selected classical optimization baselines?

6. Proposed Methodology

The project will proceed through five principal stages.

Stage I — Mathematical Foundations

Study quantum states, tensor products, Hermitian and unitary operators, quantum gates, measurement, and computational complexity. Derive the mathematical formulation of the Max-Cut cost Hamiltonian and QAOA state evolution.

Stage II — Quantum Algorithm and Circuit Design

Construct the QAOA cost and mixing operators, parameterized circuit layers, and mathematical expressions for expected optimization objectives. Analyze the associated gate counts and circuit complexity.

Stage III — Software Implementation

Implement QAOA using Python and suitable quantum computing software frameworks. Develop an independent numerical reference implementation for verifying small problem instances.

Stage IV — Experimental Evaluation

Generate structured and random graph instances. Investigate approximation ratios, optimization convergence, circuit depth, parameter initialization, and computational requirements. Compare results against classical reference algorithms.

Stage V — Analysis and Reporting

Validate experimental findings, assess limitations, document reproducibility, and prepare a complete technical research report.

7. Software and Computational Resources

The proposed implementation will use Python and appropriate scientific computing and quantum simulation libraries, potentially including NumPy, SciPy, NetworkX, Qiskit, or PennyLane.

Experiments will run in a cloud-based notebook environment such as Google Colab or Kaggle.

No physical quantum processor or specialized quantum hardware is required.

8. Expected Outcomes

The project is expected to deliver:

  • A rigorous mathematical formulation of QAOA for Max-Cut.
  • A validated software-based quantum circuit simulation framework.
  • An experimental analysis of circuit depth and parameter optimization.
  • Comparative results against selected classical algorithms.
  • An assessment of computational complexity and scalability.
  • A reproducible codebase with documented experiments.
  • A complete technical project report and presentation.

The project does not presume that QAOA will outperform classical algorithms. Both positive and negative experimental findings will be evaluated scientifically.

10. Scope and Limitations

The project will focus on software-based quantum algorithm analysis and simulation. Physical quantum hardware implementation, experimental hardware calibration, and quantum processor engineering are outside the project scope.

The primary optimization problem will be Max-Cut. Extensions to additional optimization problems will be considered only if time and computational resources permit.

Claims regarding quantum advantage will be restricted to what can be theoretically established or experimentally demonstrated under explicitly stated conditions.

12. Conclusion

This project proposes a mathematically rigorous and computationally reproducible study of the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm for combinatorial optimization.

By integrating quantum algorithm theory, circuit simulation, classical optimization, and experimental evaluation, the project aims to develop a detailed understanding of QAOA's behavior and limitations within a four-month course-project schedule.

The proposed work is intended to satisfy the academic requirements of the Quantum Algorithms course while remaining aligned with a computer science and advanced AI/ML research background.

Submitted for faculty guide review and approval.
