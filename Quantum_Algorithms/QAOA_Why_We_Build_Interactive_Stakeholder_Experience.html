Our core position: We are not inventing QAOA or competing with Qiskit. We are building a transparent, mathematically checked environment to study when QAOA works, why it struggles, and whether graph-informed parameter choices can help under fair evaluation budgets.
Question 0 · Purpose

Why are we developing this software?

Because running an algorithm and understanding its behavior are different achievements. Our project is designed to make the algorithm's mathematics, experimental conditions, and limitations inspectable.

01

Make the theory executable

Connect quantum states, Hamiltonians, and parameterized circuits to code that can be tested against known mathematical identities.

02

Explain algorithm behavior

Reveal how graph structure, circuit depth, and starting parameters change the expected quality of solutions.

03

Compare fairly

Use exact small-instance solutions and classical heuristics as baselines, while accounting for optimization evaluations and simulation cost.

Our software is a research instrument, not a commercial product promise. Its first job is to produce trustworthy explanations and reproducible evidence, not to claim that quantum methods are faster.

What changes because this software exists?

Without our research workflow

We may observe a QAOA output, but cannot easily explain whether it was caused by graph structure, parameter initialization, optimization budget, or a coding convention.

With our research workflow

We can repeat experiments, verify calculations independently, isolate one variable at a time, and explain both successful and unsuccessful results.

The motivating problem

What is Max-Cut, and why choose it?

A graph consists of vertices and weighted connections. Max-Cut asks us to assign every vertex to one of two groups, maximizing the total weight of edges crossing between the groups.

Simple story

Imagine dividing a connected network into two groups. Every connection between opposite groups earns a score. We want the largest possible score.

Weighted Max-Cut generalizes this by making some connections more important than others.

Why it is a useful benchmark

The objective is easy to calculate, small instances can be solved exactly, and the same problem can be encoded into a QAOA cost Hamiltonian. It therefore supports rigorous correctness checks.

C(z) = Σ(i,j)∈E wij (zi ⊕ zj)

The number of binary assignments is 2ⁿ; swapping the two group labels gives the same cut. The software uses exact enumeration only on small graphs.

Software architecture

How will the full research software work?

1
Define the graph

Input vertices, edges, and positive weights; generate structured and random graph families with documented seeds.

2
Encode the optimization problem

Construct HC = ½ Σ wij(I − ZiZj) and prove its basis-state eigenvalues match classical cut values.

3
Simulate QAOA

Prepare |+⟩ⁿ, alternate cost and mixer evolutions, and calculate the exact expected objective for selected circuit depths.

4
Optimize parameters

Compare random multistart, conventional optimization, and a carefully defined graph-informed initialization strategy.

5
Compare classical baselines

Use exact enumeration for small graphs and stronger classical heuristics for larger instances; normalize experimental budgets.

6
Audit and report

Export numerical results, plots, runtime records, seeds, test logs, and limitations so experiments can be independently reproduced.

Evidence standard

What will we measure to justify the work?

Solution quality

Expected cut divided by exact optimum for instances where the optimum is computable; sampled best cut reported separately.

Optimization cost

Number of objective evaluations, convergence curves, wall-clock time, and classical preprocessing effort.

Quantum resource model

Number of qubits, QAOA layers, two-qubit interactions, and idealized circuit gate counts.

Structure sensitivity

Degree statistics, triangles, weighted edges, and carefully selected graph-family comparisons.

Reproducibility

Random seeds, fixed splits, independent reference implementation, unit tests, and published configuration files.

Limitations

Simulation scales exponentially in qubit count; no quantum advantage can be inferred from small classical simulations alone.

Do not confuse metrics: expected approximation ratio, best observed sampled cut, and probability of sampling an optimum are three different measurements.

Reviewer questions

Difficult questions we should be ready to answer

Why not simply use Qiskit and stop there?

We can use Qiskit as a secondary reference, but the course learning goal includes deriving and verifying the algorithm, designing controlled experiments, and explaining performance. Calling an existing library does not answer those research questions.

What is new in this project?

The minimum course contribution is a reproducible, mathematically verified evaluation framework. A more original graph-informed parameter study is proposed but requires prior-work review before a novelty claim.

Does it solve real industrial optimization problems?

Not as a demonstrated outcome of this proposal. Weighted Max-Cut is a benchmark with connections to broader optimization formulations; real industrial performance would require separate domain validation.

Why do we need quantum simulation if we have no hardware?

Simulation allows us to inspect exact quantum states and validate circuits. It is suitable for studying small instances, but does not demonstrate hardware performance or practical quantum advantage.

Could classical methods outperform QAOA?

Yes. Strong classical methods may outperform QAOA in solution quality or computational cost. We will report that outcome rather than selectively presenting favorable cases.

Why weighted Max-Cut?

It has a clear binary objective, a direct Hamiltonian encoding, exact small-instance checks, and controllable structural variations. Weighted instances broaden the benchmark without requiring multiple unrelated optimization domains.

What is the biggest research risk?

Scope expansion and overstated novelty. We mitigate both by defining small reproducible experiments, a minimum viable mathematical result, and a separate literature review for optional extensions.

Why not use an STL file?

STL is a 3D mesh format, not an interactive document container. It cannot directly embed HTML text, controls, JavaScript simulations, or dashboards. A self-contained HTML file is the correct choice for a presentation with interactive 3D-style graphics.

## 1. What is the actual use of our project?

Imagine a company has a large network of interconnected computers, locations, or tasks. It needs to divide the network into two groups while maximizing the total importance of the connections between those groups.

This is a mathematical problem called Max-Cut.

Illustration: divide a network into two groups to maximize the total weight of connections crossing between them.

A classical computer can solve small examples exactly. Larger instances may require approximation algorithms or heuristics.

Our project investigates whether QAOA, a quantum optimization algorithm, can produce good solutions, and how its performance depends on the problem and algorithm configuration.

## 3. Then why should we create our own software?

Because our goal is different from simply releasing another quantum computing library.

Our project is an academic investigation of QAOA's behavior, with an implementation designed to make that investigation transparent, reproducible, and mathematically verifiable.

| Existing software                      | Our proposed course project                             |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Provides QAOA implementations          | Independently verifies the mathematical implementation  |
| Runs optimization algorithms           | Investigates why optimization succeeds or fails         |
| Supports quantum circuits              | Measures how circuit depth affects quality and cost     |
| Offers visualization tools             | Connects visual results to specific research questions  |
| May support many optimization problems | Focuses on a controlled weighted Max-Cut benchmark      |
| Provides general functionality         | Produces a documented, reproducible academic evaluation |

For a Level 7 course project, implementing an established algorithm and rigorously evaluating it can be academically worthwhile. The work does not automatically need to introduce a previously unknown quantum algorithm.

However, if the guide expects a novel research contribution, we must define a narrower question that existing papers have not already answered.

## 4. Where could our contribution be?

I would focus our investigation on one specific idea:

Can inexpensive structural information about a weighted graph help us select better starting parameters for QAOA under a fixed computational budget?

For example, suppose we have 100 different graph optimization problems.

Instead of optimizing QAOA completely from scratch for every graph, we could investigate whether graphs with similar structural properties can reuse or adapt previously successful parameters.

Previously studied graph

Known good QAOA parameters

New graph

Try transferring parameters

Compare parameter reuse against fresh optimization

We would compare three strategies: random initialization, a standard optimization baseline, and a graph-informed initialization strategy.

Then we would measure whether graph-informed initialization actually reduces objective evaluations or improves solution quality under equal budgets.

The underlying idea has already been studied, including graph-learning approaches. Our potential contribution would need to come from a carefully defined variation, stronger evaluation, or a new analytical result—not merely applying parameter transfer.&#x20;
